  1. CHI Play
    The Accessibility Paradox: Platform Standardisation and the Legitimacy of Play
    Jack Burnett
    In Companion Proceedings of the Annual Symposium on Computer-Human Interaction in Play, Nov 2026
    Perspectives on Play
    Abs DOI Bib Video

    Platform standardisation improves accessibility across contemporary gaming ecosystems, but it also shapes which forms of accessible play are recognised as legitimate. This Perspectives on Play paper introduces the accessibility paradox of platform standardisation: the same infrastructures that support accessibility can constrain adaptation and community-led solutions. I argue that accessibility is not solely a technical challenge but also a question of authority, legitimacy, and governance.

  2. CHI Play
    Designing with Impaired Technologies: A Game Jam of Constraints and Affordance Shrinkage
    Priscilla Y. Lo, Grace Jane Stangroome, Vishal Joshi, Jack Burnett, David Thue, Dan Bennett, and Oussama Metatla
    In Companion Proceedings of the Annual Symposium on Computer-Human Interaction in Play, Nov 2026
    Work in Progress
    Abs DOI Bib

    Playing under constraint, whether due to disability, financial limitations, technological obsolescence, or other factors, is a widespread but under-examined aspect of gaming culture. We introduce “impaired technologies” (damaged peripherals such as cracked screens, controllers with missing buttons, and faulty speakers) as design provocations to explore what happens when the affordances available for play “shrink.” Drawing on Dokumaci’s concepts of “affordance shrinkage” and “activist affordances,” we hosted a two-day public game jam where participants (n=9) first played existing games on impaired technologies, then designed their own games inspired by the experience. We identified six adaptive play strategies improvised under constraint, and three design themes, transforming “bugs” into features, embedding constraint within gameplay, and prioritizing fun over challenge. Our findings position constrained play as a site of creativity and agency, with implications for accessibility, jank, sub-optimal hardware gaming, and inclusive design.

  3. CHI Play
    Playing with Constraints: Designing Inclusive Games and Controllers
    Priscilla Y. Lo, Grace Stangroome, Vishal Joshi, Jack Burnett, David Thue, and Oussama Metatla
    In Companion Proceedings of the Annual Symposium on Computer-Human Interaction in Play, Nov 2026
    Workshop Proposal
    Abs DOI Bib Website

    How can we design inclusive games that appeal to gamers with different abilities? This workshop invites the CHI PLAY community to play with constraints and conceptualize games that enable forms of play beyond conventional patterns. We build on our prior work that applies the concept of “impaired technologies” as a design provocation in a game jam to encourage alternative perspectives on play. Participants will create multiplayer games that are played with impaired technologies or, as an extension, that use novel interactions. We aim to engage researchers and practitioners interested in designing playful prototypes, including digital games, immersive experiences, tangible toys, and game controllers.
